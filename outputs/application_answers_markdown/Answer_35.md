Please identify an issue currently in the mainstream media and explain how this is likely to impact the clients that HFW represents and the type of work that the firm undertakes (350 words) One issue that has and will continue to impact HFW and its clients is the rising tensions between China and Taiwan. It is thought that China's current military assertiveness around Taiwan may eventually become a full-scale invasion at some point over the coming months. Pertinent to HFW's shipping sector of expertise, events in the region have led major shipping groups to seek to incorporate break clauses into existing contracts with Chinese shipbuilders. These clauses would, in theory, allow western businesses to walk away from deals if western governments were to impose sanctions on China. Sanctions imposed on Russia as a result of its invasion of Ukraine were extensive and sudden, so businesses are expressing an eagerness to ensure they hedge themselves against trade regulation risk if similar measures are imposed by western governments on China. However, industry experts have emphasised the difficulty of this process. Many of China's shipbuilding companies are state-owned including China State Shipbuilding Corporation; one of the largest and most influential in the world. This makes the process of incorporating clauses that allow companies to walk away from deals nearly impossible as the government simply won't agree, especially given that many businesses are already contracted into long-term deals. In light of the issues facing shipping companies in relation to China, I believe it represents an exciting opportunity for HFW to meet the demand for advice in this area. Following the broad interpretation of sanctions clauses in Lamesa Investments Limited v Cynergy Bank Limited, the firm can assist clients by incorporating sanctions clauses into contracts which will enable them to operate in line with domestic law without breaching contractual terms. Although it may be difficult to implement clauses that allow companies to rescind contracts, there is also a scope for HFW, through its trade sanctions expertise and market-leading shipping practice, to negotiate alternative solutions for clients. This could include incorporating contractual provisions that allow for compensation in the event of domestic sanctions, for example. Clients seeking sanctions clauses will require a focus on careful and precise drafting which HFW is undoubtedly well-equipped to offer.
