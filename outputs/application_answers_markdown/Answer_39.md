Please explain your motivations for pursuing a career as a commercial solicitor and why you think Norton Rose Fulbright would be a good match for you (Maximum 750 words). My interest in commercial law began during my time at Schroders, when my team was leading the compliance process with new European sustainability regulations. After reviewing a 200-page consultation paper, the directors in my team were still uncertain about the regulatory implications for our funds. The project progressed much quicker because the City lawyers advising us effectively explained the specific changes impacting our firm. I was so impressed by the lawyers' articulate nature and tailored advice that I explored the idea of becoming a solicitor myself. I confirmed this interest upon attending insight events at Paul Hastings, Baker McKenzie and Vinson & Elkins. When I was working on a case study on an international joint venture, I found myself supplementing my legal arguments with my understanding of Russian foreign policy and private equity investments. As someone with a wide range of academic interests, I really enjoyed how topical and multidisciplinary the analysis is in commercial law, especially compared to the heavily quantitative work in my former role. I believe law allows me to develop my existing interest in business and finance while capitalising on my natural strengths as a History and International Relations graduate. Above all, Norton Rose Fulbright's multicultural environment compelled me to apply. At the Energy Transition event, senior associate Melusi Dlamini shared that the firm's wide international reach allowed them to explore their interest in African energy work. As a Hongkonger that attended an international school with over 80 nationalities represented, I am eager to work with people from different regions. My roommates were from Bulgaria, Timor-Leste, and Mauritius respectively, and I learnt a lot from hearing their different perspectives on global issues. My knowledge of and passion for different cultures would be well-utilised and further developed at such a global firm. I am confident in my ability to build rapport with international clients, particularly in the international secondment. Secondly, I am drawn to Norton Rose Fulbright's unique industry focus, particularly its strengths in energy. From engaging with energy-related case studies and speaking with project financing lawyers at the Vinson & Elkins, I really enjoyed the tangibility of energy work. Partner Amy Armitage echoed this, where she said seeing the physical sites she worked on felt highly rewarding. The industry-focused structure means trainees can learn from lawyers with deep sectoral expertise and see projects throughout the whole lifecycle. Also, the firm's practice group has won not just accolades in their local markets, but also global Band 1 rankings for multi-jurisdictional work. This makes Norton Rose Fulbright the ideal place for me to explore potential areas I may specialise in upon qualification as a solicitor at an international law firm. I believe I have the key competencies to succeed as a commercial lawyer. Firstly, I am capable of dissecting complex information and making insightful observations. I produced a monthly firmwide update on the cross-border retail fund market, which the CEO often engaged with. From large datasets, I extract relevant data points to highlight top-selling sectors and funds, explaining trends and outliers with macroeconomic analysis. This required a high standard of analytical rigour, which is foundational to legal work. Secondly, I always take the initiative to learn and help others. My manager was on secondment and only worked Mondays and Fridays. I familiarised myself with the data and slides he regularly updates for the Sustainability ExCo in case queries come in mid-week. Later when there were urgent requests from the Chief Investment Officer, I was able to respond and support my manager and team. This shows that I am not afraid to challenge myself with greater responsibilities, and I proactively think of ways to support the team. I am also a self-starter that remains resilient in difficult circumstances. During my internship at BlackRock (made virtual during Covid), there was an 8-hour time difference as I was home in Hong Kong. Despite these setbacks, I was determined to maximise my learning as I lacked prior knowledge of finance compared to the economics students in my cohort. I used all means available to me, ranging from having coffee chats with 30 people across geographies and functions to self-studying the Bloomberg Market Concepts certification. I believe I have the grit to thrive in the high-stakes environment of a global law firm like Norton Rose Fulbright. (712 words)
