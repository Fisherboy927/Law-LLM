## Introduce us to a topic you know about and explain why it interests you (300 words) The increasing use of loyalty programmes within branding and marketing strategies has piqued my interest. These schemes reflect a fascinating psychological phenomenon wherein the promise of, and sense of ownership over, rewards, often motivates consumers to increase or expedite expenditure. As FT's Brooke Masters aptly concluded on a recent Financial Times News Briefing Episode, when examining the increasing use and success of such schemes within the airline industry, "people will pay anything to get something for free." She highlighted that IAG (parent company of British Airways) generates more revenue from its rewards currency, Avios, than it does from its airlines, as consumers, eager to receive what they consider to be a bargain, subconsciously spend more frivolously. This phenomenon interests me as it starkly contrasts with political economist J.S. Mill's 'homo economicus' model which I studied in A Level Economics, which suggested that consumers make decisions based purely on cost-benefit analyses. Loyalty programmes overhaul this theory, tapping into deeper psychological drivers, including the desire for exclusivity or the fear of missing out. Additionally, positive reinforcement – the psychological phenomenon wherein favourable outcomes are attached to specific actions – occurs when businesses offer exclusive rewards for profitable customer behaviour, further contradicting consumer rationality. I also find this trend fascinating due to examining the phenomenon's impact on my weekly grocery shop. The introduction of Lidl's 'Lidl Plus' scheme, which competes with Tesco's well-established 'Tesco Clubcard', has noticeably influenced my own spending habits, leading me to explore new shops. This exploration allowed me to realise the stark difference in pricing, leading to a shift in my shopping habits and ultimately severing my former brand loyalty to Tesco. This first-hand experience deepens my interest in the strategies companies use to develop competitive advantage and customer engagement in crowded markets.
